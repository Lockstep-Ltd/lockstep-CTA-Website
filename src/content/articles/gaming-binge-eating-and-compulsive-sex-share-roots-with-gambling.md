Scientific Inquirer, a science news site, reports on a wide-ranging study of how other compulsive behaviours compare with gambling disorder. Its write-up draws on Flinders University's announcement of the research, and adds a careful reading of what the findings do and don't show.

The study, published in *European Psychiatry*, brings together researchers from Université Paris Cité and Hôpital Sainte-Anne in France, Flinders University in Australia, Semmelweis University in Hungary, the University of Gibraltar and Aga Khan University. Rather than recruiting patients, they pooled almost 250 systematic reviews and meta-analyses. They then compared alcohol use disorder and six behavioural addictions with gambling disorder across 75 factors, from mental health and personality to emotional regulation, cognition and brain function. Gaming disorder came out 98.9% similar, followed by compulsive sexual behaviour and binge eating.

Scientific Inquirer rightly urges caution with that figure. It reflects what earlier research happened to measure, and it doesn't mean that heavy gaming is the same as harmful gambling.

The researchers' message is a hopeful one. As Professor Zsolt Demetrovics and Andrea Czakó of Flinders University explain, recognising the loss of control these behaviours share can ease shame, open up conversations, and help people find support before serious harm is done.
